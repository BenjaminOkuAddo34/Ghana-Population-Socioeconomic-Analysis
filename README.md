## 1. Project Overview

**Ghana Population & Socioeconomic Data Analysis** is a data analytics and geospatial analysis project focused on exploring population distribution, demographic characteristics and socioeconomic patterns across Ghana.

The project aims to transform demographic and socioeconomic datasets into meaningful insights through data collection, cleaning, exploratory data analysis, statistical analysis, visualization and geospatial mapping.

Using Python and GIS technologies, the project will investigate how population is distributed across Ghana, identify differences between regions and districts, calculate population density, and examine how population patterns relate to selected socioeconomic indicators.

The analysis is organised into distinct analytical modules, beginning with population distribution and density before progressing into demographic and socioeconomic characteristics.

By combining statistical analysis with geographic information, the project seeks to demonstrate how data analytics and GIS can support planning, resource allocation and evidence-based decision-making.

## 2. Problem Statement

Population and socioeconomic information are important for understanding the demographic and developmental characteristics of a country.

However, raw demographic datasets are not always easy to interpret, particularly when information comes from different sources, contains inconsistencies or lacks geographic context.

Understanding population distribution alone does not provide a complete picture of regional development. Factors such as education, employment, housing conditions, access to basic services and demographic characteristics also contribute to understanding differences between communities.

This project seeks to address these challenges by collecting, cleaning, integrating and analysing publicly available demographic, socioeconomic and geospatial datasets for Ghana.

The analysis will provide insights into population distribution, regional disparities and selected socioeconomic patterns through statistical summaries, visualizations and thematic maps.

## 7. Project Methodology

The project will follow a structured analytical workflow.

### Stage 1: Data Collection

Collect relevant datasets from Ghana Statistical Service and other publicly available sources.

Potential datasets include:

- National and regional population statistics.
- District-level population statistics.
- Population by age and sex.
- Urban and rural population distribution.
- Education and literacy indicators.

- Administrative boundary shapefiles or GeoJSON files.

### Stage 2: Data Inspection and Cleaning

Use Pandas, NumPy and other relevant Python libraries to prepare datasets for analysis.

Activities include:

- Inspecting dataset structures.
- Identifying missing values.
- Detecting duplicates.
- Correcting inconsistent data formats.
- Standardising regional and district names.
- Converting columns to appropriate data types.
- Handling invalid or incomplete records.
- Preparing datasets for integration.

### Stage 3: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Explore datasets to understand their characteristics and identify patterns.

Analysis will include:

- Descriptive statistics.
- Population summaries.
- Regional and district comparisons.
- Distribution analysis.
- Identification of unusual values.
- Historical comparisons where comparable data is available.
- Initial exploration of socioeconomic indicators.

### Stage 4: Statistical Analysis

Calculate relevant demographic and socioeconomic indicators.

Potential indicators include:

**Population Density**

Population density measures the number of people living within a given area.

Population Density = Total Population / Land Area (km²)

**Population Growth Rate**

Population growth rate measures the percentage change in population between two comparable periods.

Population Growth Rate (%) = ((Current Population - Previous Population) / Previous Population) × 100

**Urban Population Percentage**

Urban Population (%) = (Urban Population / Total Population) × 100

**Population Share**

Population Share (%) = (Area Population / National Population) × 100

Additional indicators will depend on the availability and consistency of the datasets.

### Stage 5: Geospatial Analysis

Use GeoPandas, Rasterio and QGIS to integrate demographic information with Ghana's administrative boundaries and gridded datasets.

Activities include:

- Loading administrative boundary datasets.
- Inspecting geographic coordinate reference systems.
- Cleaning and validating geographic data.
- Joining population statistics to administrative boundaries.
- Calculating geographic area measurements using appropriate projected coordinate systems.
- Calculating population density.
- Processing and clipping raster datasets to Ghana's geographic extent.
- Creating regional and district-level choropleth maps.
- Analysing spatial population patterns.

### Stage 6: Data Visualization

Use Matplotlib and Seaborn to communicate findings.

Planned visualizations include:

- Population by region.
- Population by district and administrative area.
- Population density by region and district.
- Gridded population distribution.
- Population growth comparisons.
- Age and sex distribution charts.
- Urban versus rural population comparisons.
- Socioeconomic indicator comparisons.

### Stage 7: Reporting and Dashboard Development

Use Microsoft Excel and structured documentation to communicate important findings.

The dashboard will include:

- Key population indicators.
- Regional comparisons.
- Statistical charts and visual summaries.
- Population density indicators.
- Selected demographic and socioeconomic indicators.

A final analytical report will explain the methodology, findings, limitations and recommendations.

## 12. Project Limitations and Considerations

- The analysis will depend on the availability and quality of publicly accessible datasets.
- Different datasets may use different administrative boundary definitions.
- Historical comparisons must account for changes in administrative boundaries and census methodology.
- Population estimates may differ from official census counts.
- Population density calculations depend on accurate geographic area measurements.
- Gridded population datasets are modelled spatial estimates and may differ from census administrative totals.
- Correlation between socioeconomic variables does not automatically imply causation.
- Geographic analysis will depend on the accuracy and resolution of available spatial datasets.
- Some socioeconomic indicators may only be available at regional rather than district level.
- District and regional densities use the area of the district boundary polygons, while the World Bank uses land area and its own population estimates. Ghana's national density is therefore about 129 per km² in the maps and 142.9 per km² in the international comparison.
- The WorldPop raster total may differ from the census total because it is a modelled estimate; this difference is to be quantified and documented.
